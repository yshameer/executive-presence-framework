# Example 2 — Saying no to your director on Slack

**Skill:** `executive-presence` · **Mode:** written · **Stakes:** low ceremony, real consequence

Shows **calibration**. Same skill as [Example 1](01-bad-news-to-leadership.md), a fraction of the output. A Slack reply doesn't get a five-part treatment, and there's no delivery note because nobody is speaking.

---

## The ask

> my director just slacked me: "quick one - can we pull the analytics dashboard work into this sprint?" we're already at capacity, the team is doing the SOC2 remediation which has a hard audit date in 5 weeks. what do i say

---

## The response

### Diagnosis

This is a two-line answer that people turn into a paragraph. You have a hard external date and a fixed team — the no writes itself. The only real risk is hedging your way into an accidental yes.

### Frameworks

**Decisive Answering** plus **C·D·S**. That's it — the audit date is the data and it does the persuading for you.

### The words

> No — the team's fully on SOC2 remediation and the audit date is [date], 5 weeks out. Pulling the dashboard in puts that at risk.
>
> Happy to start it [date after audit], or if the dashboard is more urgent than the audit, tell me and I'll re-plan around that.

### Coaching note

That last line is the whole move: you're not refusing, you're handing the trade-off back to the person who owns it. A "no" that names an alternative and offers to be overruled on priority reads as ownership. A "no" with three sentences of explanation about how busy the team is reads as complaining — and invites negotiation on effort rather than a decision on priority.

## What to notice

| | Example 1 | Example 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Sections used | All five | Diagnosis, frameworks, words, note |
| Frameworks | 4 | 2 |

The skill reads the scenario for how much weight it carries. Over-engineering a Slack reply wastes your time and makes the frameworks feel bureaucratic — so it doesn't.

If you get more ceremony than a moment deserves, say **"shorter."**
